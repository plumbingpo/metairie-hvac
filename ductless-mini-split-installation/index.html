A ductless mini split solves the problems central systems cannot touch: the garage workshop that bakes all summer, the addition the ductwork never reached, the bonus room that is never quite comfortable, the older Metairie, LA house with no ducts at all. Each indoor head connects to its outdoor unit through a small line set carrying refrigerant, power, and a condensate drain, and with no ducts there are no duct losses. The inverter-driven compressor ramps smoothly up and down instead of slamming on and off, holding temperature within a fraction of a degree while running at whisper volume. In heating mode, a modern mini split is a genuine cold-climate heat pump, not just an air conditioner with ambitions.

Most disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The most common error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and never settles into a steady rhythm. Metairie HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity makes sense across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Metairie home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it moves air well and still looks right, with outdoor line sets covered in finished line-hide rather than left bare.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a measured, micron-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since flare joints are where careless installs leak. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings actually explained. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Most failed mini splits we see in Metairie come from installs that treated the job as simple. The flares are where it usually goes wrong: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head blows warm. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves contamination inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs steal capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will drip down the wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Metairie HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of one that mysteriously underperforms.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Rinse or wash the indoor unit's filter screens monthly; they load up faster than furnace filters
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
