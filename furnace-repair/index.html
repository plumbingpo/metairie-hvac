From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Metairie homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we work on them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. That is why the majority of no-heat calls end the same visit. If your furnace is down anywhere in Metairie, call (504) 531-6049 and we will get the burners lit today.

Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower sends through your home. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

At Metairie HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to catch airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
